# Presentation Outline: Understanding SN1, SN2, E1, and E2

> **Audience:** A beginner student who has never studied organic mechanisms before.  
> **Rule Reference:** `AGENTS.md` Section 10 (Teaching Slide Workflow) & Section 11 (Language & Writing Style Rules).  
> **Required Structure:** Hook → Why it matters → Key vocabulary → SN2 → SN1 → E2 → E1 → Comparison → How to choose → Practice → Summary.

---

## Slide 1: Hook — What Happens When Molecules Meet?
- **Learning Goal:** Introduce the two main events in organic chemistry: swapping parts and making double bonds.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/index.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. Organic molecules react to change their shapes and jobs.
  2. A molecule can swap one piece for another (substitution).
  3. A molecule can lose pieces to make a double bond (elimination).
  4. Four famous pathways control these reactions: SN1, SN2, E1, and E2.

---

## Slide 2: Why It Matters — Real Uses of These Reactions
- **Learning Goal:** Explain why learning these four mechanisms matters in real life.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/index.md` (section "Why It Matters", added in the fix run because the first outline used facts not in the wiki)
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. Alkyl halides are used as solvents, anesthetics, and refrigerants.
  2. Alcohols are used as fuels, solvents, and antiseptics.
  3. Chemists use these reactions to build bigger molecules.
  4. The wrong reaction gives a different product, such as an alkene.
  5. By the end, you will predict which path wins.

---

## Slide 3: Key Vocabulary — Meet the Reaction Team
- **Learning Goal:** Define the main chemical terms in plain words before showing reactions.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/index.md`, `wiki/sn2.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **Substrate:** The starting carbon molecule that undergoes the reaction.
  2. **Leaving Group (LG):** A piece that leaves and takes bond electrons with it.
  3. **Nucleophile:** An electron-rich piece that attacks a positive carbon atom.
  4. **Base:** A piece that takes a hydrogen ion (proton) from a carbon atom.

---

## Slide 4: Key Vocabulary — Substrate Crowding (1°, 2°, 3°)
- **Learning Goal:** Teach how carbon atoms are classified by crowding.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/sn2.md`, `wiki/sn1.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **Methyl (0°):** The reactive carbon connects to zero other carbons.
  2. **Primary (1°):** The reactive carbon connects to only one other carbon.
  3. **Secondary (2°):** The reactive carbon connects to two other carbons.
  4. **Tertiary (3°):** The reactive carbon connects to three other carbons (very crowded).

---

## Slide 5: SN2 — One Step, Backside Attack
- **Learning Goal:** Explain the SN2 substitution pathway and its one-step nature.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/sn2.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **SN2** stands for Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular.
  2. Everything happens in one single, fast step without any intermediate.
  3. The nucleophile attacks from the back (180° away from the leaving group).
  4. As the new bond forms, the leaving group pushed out simultaneously.
  5. The speed depends on both the substrate and the nucleophile: `Rate = k[substrate][Nu]`.

---

## Slide 6: SN2 — The Umbrella Flip and Open Paths
- **Learning Goal:** Explain stereochemical inversion and why SN2 loves uncrowded carbons.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/sn2.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. Backside attack flips the three remaining bonds like an umbrella in wind.
  2. This 100% flip of 3D shape is called **Walden inversion**.
  3. Crowded carbons block the nucleophile from reaching the back face.
  4. Reactivity order: Methyl > Primary (1°) > Secondary (2°) >>> Tertiary (3°) is blocked.
  5. Polar aprotic solvents (like acetone) keep the nucleophile naked and fast.

---

## Slide 7: SN1 — Break First, Attach Later
- **Learning Goal:** Explain the two-step SN1 substitution pathway and carbocation formation.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/sn1.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **SN1** stands for Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular.
  2. The reaction takes two separate steps instead of one.
  3. **Step 1 (Slow):** The leaving group falls off, forming a **carbocation**.
  4. **Carbocation:** A flat carbon with three bonds and a positive charge.
  5. The speed depends only on the substrate: `Rate = k[substrate]`.

---

## Slide 8: SN1 — Flat Intermediate and Mixed Products
- **Learning Goal:** Explain why SN1 gives racemization and prefers 3° carbons.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/sn1.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **Step 2 (Fast):** The nucleophile attacks the flat carbocation.
  2. The nucleophile can attack from the top face or bottom face equally.
  3. Attacking both faces gives a 50:50 mixture of 3D shapes (**racemization**).
  4. Tertiary (3°) carbons make the most stable carbocations.
  5. Polar protic solvents (like water or ethanol) help the leaving group break away.

---

## Slide 9: E2 — The One-Step Team Elimination
- **Learning Goal:** Introduce the E2 elimination pathway to create double bonds.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/e2.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **E2** stands for Elimination Bimolecular.
  2. It makes a carbon–carbon double bond (alkene) in one concerted step.
  3. A strong base pulls off a neighboring beta-hydrogen (β-H).
  4. At the exact same time, the leaving group departs and the double bond forms.
  5. Speed depends on both substrate and base: `Rate = k[substrate][base]`.

---

## Slide 10: E2 — The Anti-Periplanar Line and Base Size
- **Learning Goal:** Explain the geometric requirement and regiochemical control in E2.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/e2.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. The β-H and the leaving group must face opposite sides (**anti-periplanar 180°**).
  2. A small base takes the most crowded H to make the more stable alkene (**Zaitsev rule**).
  3. A bulky base cannot fit inside, so it takes an outer H (**Hofmann rule**).
  4. E2 works well on tertiary (3°) and secondary (2°) carbons.
  5. Heat strongly favors E2 over substitution reactions.

---

## Slide 11: E1 — Two Steps to a Double Bond
- **Learning Goal:** Explain the E1 elimination pathway and how it shares the SN1 intermediate.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/e1.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **E1** stands for Elimination Unimolecular.
  2. **Step 1 (Slow):** The leaving group departs to form a carbocation (same as SN1).
  3. **Step 2 (Fast):** A weak base takes a β-hydrogen to form an alkene.
  4. Speed depends only on the substrate: `Rate = k[substrate]`.
  5. E1 always competes with SN1; adding high heat promotes the E1 alkene.

---

## Slide 12: Comparison — The Big Picture
- **Learning Goal:** Compare all four mechanisms across steps, rates, and 3D shapes.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/index.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **SN2:** 1 step, substitution, backside inversion, prefers 1° carbons.
  2. **SN1:** 2 steps, substitution, carbocation, racemization, prefers 3° carbons.
  3. **E2:** 1 step, elimination, anti-periplanar, needs strong base, makes alkene.
  4. **E1:** 2 steps, elimination, carbocation, needs weak base and heat, makes alkene.

---

## Slide 13: How to Choose — The 3-Step Decision Tree
- **Learning Goal:** Teach a simple decision method to predict the reaction.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/index.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **Check 1 (Crowding):** Is the carbon 1°, 2°, or 3°? (1° favors SN2; 3° is too crowded for SN2).
  2. **Check 2 (Reagent):** Is it a strong base/nucleophile or a weak one?
  3. Strong reagents drive 1-step paths (SN2 or E2); weak reagents drive 2-step paths (SN1 or E1).
  4. **Check 3 (Heat):** High heat favors elimination (E1/E2).

---

## Slide 14: Practice — Test Your Prediction Skills
- **Learning Goal:** Walk through beginner practice problems step-by-step.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/sn2.md`, `wiki/e2.md`, `wiki/sn1.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. **Problem 1:** 1-bromobutane (1°) + NaCN (strong Nu) → **SN2** (fast swap).
  2. **Problem 2:** 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (3°) + NaOEt (strong base) + heat → **E2** (alkene).
  3. **Problem 3:** 2-bromo-2-methylpropane (3°) + water (weak Nu) at room temp → **SN1** (alcohol).
  4. Following the 3 checks gives the correct answer every time.

---

## Slide 15: Summary — Your Reaction Cheat Sheet
- **Learning Goal:** Review the core rules before ending the lesson.
- **Wiki Source:** `wiki/index.md`
- **Planned Bullets:**
  1. Bimolecular (2) reactions happen in 1 step; Unimolecular (1) reactions take 2 steps.
  2. Uncrowded carbons favor SN2; crowded carbons favor SN1, E1, or E2.
  3. Strong bases force elimination (E2); weak bases allow carbocations (SN1/E1).
  4. Heat is the magic switch that turns substitutions into eliminations.
  5. You now know the four foundational mechanisms of organic chemistry!
