# Alkenes — Where SN1/SN2/E1/E2 Show Up in Alkene Chemistry

> Source feed: `raw/ch 8.pdf` (Wade, Chapter 8) and the elimination sections of `raw/Organic Chemistry ch 5-7.pdf`. This note only records links to the four core mechanisms. See [index](index.md).

## Overview

Alkenes are made by elimination ([E1](e1.md) or [E2](e2.md)) and react mainly by electrophilic addition. Several addition reactions reuse ideas from substitution: a carbocation intermediate (as in SN1), or a back-side attack (as in SN2).

## Kinetics & Rate Law

No new rate laws are introduced. Electrophilic addition of HX, HBr or H₂O/H⁺ goes through a carbocation, so it is a stepwise process like [SN1](sn1.md). Use the rate law of the mechanism being borrowed.

## Step-by-Step Mechanism

- **Addition of HX (Markovnikov):** the alkene is protonated to give a carbocation, then the halide attacks. This resembles the second step of SN1. Rearrangements are possible.
- **Acid-catalyzed hydration:** Markovnikov orientation, rearrangements are possible.
- **Oxymercuration–demercuration:** Markovnikov orientation, no rearrangement.
- **Hydroboration–oxidation:** anti-Markovnikov orientation, syn addition, no rearrangement.
- **Halogen addition (Br₂):** a bromonium ion forms. The nucleophile must attack from the back side, similar to SN2.
- **Williamson synthesis check (ch 8 problem):** an alkoxide attacking a primary halide by SN2 is better than attacking a secondary halide.

## Stereochemistry

- Back-side attack on the halonium ion gives **anti addition**: Br₂ + cyclopentene → trans-1,2-dibromocyclopentane (the cis product is not formed).
- Hydroboration and catalytic hydrogenation give **syn addition**.
- When achiral reactants give chiral products, racemic mixtures are produced.

## Controlling Factors

- A reaction that goes through a carbocation can rearrange. A reaction that avoids a carbocation (oxymercuration, hydroboration) does not.
- The base conditions decide the elimination pathway to make an alkene: basic conditions favor E2 (dehydrohalogenation), acidic conditions favor E1 (ch 8 reaction map).
- Which alkene forms follows [Zaitsev's rule](e1.md) unless a bulky base is used ([Hofmann product](e2.md)).
